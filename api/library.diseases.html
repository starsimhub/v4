library.diseases

library.diseases

Example disease models: cholera, COVID, dengue, Ebola, gonorrhea, HIV, influenza, malaria, measles, pertussis, polio, RSV, syphilis, TB, typhoid, and a non-communicable disease (NCD), and Starsim 3’s SIR, SEIR, and SIS written as agent-based code.

These are illustrative, lightly-parameterized models intended as starting points for building your own disease modules, not as validated models for any specific setting. Each one says in its docstring where its structure and its default parameters came from. Core disease base classes (ss.Disease, ss.AgentDisease, ss.SIR, etc.) live in starsim.diseases.

ART

library.diseases.ART(
    coverage=1.0,
    who=None,
    when=None,
    art_delay=ss.years(1),
    name=None,
    **kwargs,
)

Antiretroviral therapy, delivered as ss.treat() delivers a treatment

By default, ART is offered once to each person who has been infected with HIV for art_delay and is not on it, with the probability coverage; who= restricts who among them is offered it, and when= when, as for any delivery (see ss.Delivery). The people treated are on ART for good: their CD4 count recovers, and they are less infectious (see HIV). Requires the HIV module.

Note: CRN safe.

kwargs dict passed to ss.Delivery (e.g. capacity=, name=)

AgentSEIR

library.diseases.AgentSEIR(pars=None, dur_exp=_, **kwargs)

An SEIR model written as agent-based code rather than declared: Starsim 3’s ss.SEIR (see ssl.AgentSIR), with its lognormal dur_exp and dur_inf and p_death=0.01.

This class extends AgentSIR with an exposed state: agents who are infected but not yet infectious. exposed and infectious are the literal E and I compartments, and infected is derived from them: an agent is infected from the moment it acquires the infection, whether or not it is yet transmitting. So infected and prevalence count E plus I, while transmission depends on infectious alone.

Correspondingly, ti_exposed is the time of acquisition and ti_infectious is the time of becoming infectious. ti_infected is deliberately not defined, since it is too easily confused with ti_infectious.

Note: CRN safe.

p_death float/ss.bernoulli the probability of death from infection

AgentSIR

library.diseases.AgentSIR(
    pars=None,
    beta=_,
    init_prev=_,
    dur_inf=_,
    p_death=_,
    **kwargs,
)

An SIR model written as code: Starsim 3’s ss.SIR

This class implements a basic SIR model with states for susceptible, infected/infectious, and recovered, as per-agent arrays updated in code (see ss.AgentDisease). It also includes deaths, and basic results. It is a teaching example, showing in code what the declared ss.SIR does as agents, and a starting point for a disease written as code (e.g. ssl.HIV).

Its defaults are Starsim 3’s: a lognormal dur_inf (a mean of 6 years, std 1 year) and p_death=0.01, where the declared ss.SIR is the textbook SIR, with an exponential dur_inf and no deaths. Use it to keep a Starsim 3 model’s parameters; its timing is the agent engine’s (see above), so each case is infectious for half a step longer on average than in Starsim 3.

Note: CRN safe.

COVID

library.diseases.COVID(ages=True, **kwargs)

COVID-19, as a SECIR model by age: a chain of increasingly severe states, each of which some people leave by recovering

People who are infected are exposed (E), then infected without symptoms (INS), and then either recover or develop symptoms (ISy); of those, some become severe (ISev, in hospital), then critical (ICr, in intensive care), and some of those die. People without symptoms and with symptoms are both infectious, at rel_ins and rel_sym of the transmission rate.

The disease is stratified by age into MEmilio’s six groups (0-4, 5-14, 15-34, 35-59, 60-79, and 80+), since the severity of COVID-19 rises steeply with age: in the oldest group, a symptomatic case is 30 times as likely to become severe as in a child, and a critical one 12 times as likely to die. The fraction of people in each group is the world’s, unless the sim’s population says otherwise (e.g. people=ss.population('Germany')).

Source: the values for Germany of MEmilio (Kühn MJ et al., Mathematical Biosciences 2021, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mbs.2021.108648; examples/ode_secir_parameter_study_graph.cpp), which gives each as a range, by age; each default here is the middle of its range. MEmilio’s transmission is a contact rate times a chance of infection per contact, which is 0.06 for adults and from 0.03 (under 5) to 0.175 (80 and over), here the relative susceptibility rel_sus (1 for adults). Without contacts given, beta is 1 a day, which with the world’s ages gives an R0 of about 3.2, as for the first strains of COVID-19. With a contact matrix, beta is per contact, and MEmilio’s is 0.06: ssl.COVID(beta=0.06) with networks=ss.contacts('Germany').

With ages=False, the model has one group, with the values of MEmilio’s simple example (ode_secir_simple.cpp), whose beta of 1 a day is one contact a day, each certain to infect; with people infectious at rel_ins for 2 days and at rel_sym for 6, this gives an R0 of about 2.7.

Note: CRN safe as agents.

Parameters

Name Type Description Default

rel_sus float/ss.by the susceptibility of each age group, relative to adults’
rel_ins float the infectiousness of people without symptoms, relative to beta
rel_sym float the infectiousness of people with symptoms, relative to beta
dur_exp ss.Duration the mean time from infection to being infectious
dur_ins ss.Duration the mean time without symptoms
dur_sym ss.Duration/ss.by the mean time with symptoms
dur_sev ss.Duration/ss.by the mean time with severe disease
dur_crit ss.Duration/ss.by the mean time with critical disease
p_sym float/ss.by the fraction of people without symptoms who go on to have symptoms
p_sev float/ss.by the fraction of people with symptoms whose disease becomes severe

kwargs dict passed to ss.Disease, e.g. init or name

Examples

import starsim.library as ssl

Cholera

library.diseases.Cholera(
    *args,
    pars=None,
    strains=None,
    stratify=None,
    solver=None,
    _names=None,
    **kwargs,
)

Cholera, with both direct and environmental (waterborne) transmission

People infected are latent (E) for dur_exp, then a fraction p_symp become symptomatic (Is), and the rest are asymptomatic (Ia), shedding far less (asymp_trans). Asymptomatic people recover; symptomatic people recover, or die (with probability p_death, sooner). Besides transmission from person to person (beta), infectious people shed bacteria into a well-mixed reservoir of water (W, a count of bacteria, not people) at shedding_rate, the bacteria die off at decay_rate, and susceptible people are infected from the water at a rate that rises with the concentration, beta_env times env_conc = W/(W + half_sat_rate), as in the model of Codeço (2001). It is declared, so it runs under any solver.

Sources: Codeço CT, “Endemic and epidemic dynamics of cholera: the role of the aquatic reservoir”, BMC Infect Dis 2001;1:1 (https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2334-1-1), for the structure; the latent period is from Azman et al. (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3677557/), a lognormal; asymptomatic shedding of 1 to 10 days (uniform) from the WHO fact sheet; 5 days of symptoms (a lognormal fit to the range of 2.9 to 14 days) from Fung (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3926264/); a rapid death of severe cases (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5767916/); half of infections symptomatic, mid range of Jackson et al. and Nelson et al.; asymptomatic carriers shedding 100 times less; the dose-response, shedding, and decay from Mukandavire et al. (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3102413/). beta (from person to person) and beta_env are illustrative. Since the bacteria are a count, shed by everyone infectious into one reservoir, the water carries more of the epidemic in a larger population: with these values, it sustains an outbreak in a town of 10,000, and transmission from person to person alone does not.

Note: CRN safe as agents.

Parameters

Name Type Description Default
beta ss.per the rate of transmission from person to person (per contact, if there are networks), for a symptomatic person
asymp_trans float the relative transmissibility and shedding of asymptomatic people
beta_env ss.per the rate of infection from the water, when it is saturated with bacteria
half_sat_rate float the amount of bacteria at which infection from the water is half its maximum
shedding_rate ss.freq the bacteria each symptomatic person sheds into the water, per unit time
decay_rate ss.per the rate at which bacteria in the water die off
dur_exp ss.Duration/ss.Dist the time from infection to being infectious (a duration is the mean of an exponential)
dur_asymp2rec ss.Duration/ss.Dist the time asymptomatic people are infectious
dur_symp2rec ss.Duration/ss.Dist the time symptomatic people are ill, for those who recover
dur_symp2dead ss.Duration/ss.Dist the time from symptoms to death, for those who die
p_symp float the probability that an infection is symptomatic
p_death float the probability that a symptomatic person dies
kwargs dict passed to ss.Disease, e.g. init_prev, init or name

Examples

import starsim as ss
import starsim.library as ssl

sim = ss.Sim(diseases=ssl.Cholera(), total_pop=10_000, start='2025-01-01', stop='2026-01-01')
sim.run()
sim.cholera.plot()

Dengue

library.diseases.Dengue(n_serotypes=4, cross=0.3, init_prev=0.001, **kwargs)

Dengue: several serotypes, each passed on by mosquitoes, with partial cross-protection between them

Each serotype is a disease of its own (ssl.DengueSerotype), den1 to den4, so this is a set of diseases, given to a sim as one (ss.Sim(diseases=ssl.Dengue())), and the results are sim.results.den1 and so on. Having had one serotype gives complete protection against that one and partial protection against the others (cross), a modifier of each serotype’s rel_sus.

Sources: the host-vector structure is the Ross-Macdonald model as given by Keeling MJ, Rohani P, Modeling Infectious Diseases in Humans and Animals, Princeton University Press, 2008, section 4.4 (see ssl.Malaria). The dengue values are indicative ones from the literature rather than one source’s fitted set: about 6 days of viremia in people and an extrinsic incubation period of about 10 days in Aedes aegypti at tropical temperatures, with a mosquito lifetime of about 10 days. That infection with one serotype gives lasting protection against it and temporary, partial protection against the others is the finding of Reich NG et al., “Interactions between serotypes of dengue highlight epidemiological impact of cross-immunity”, J R Soc Interface 2013;10(86):20130414 (https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2013.0414); here that cross-protection does not wane, which is a simplification, and its size (cross) is illustrative.

Note: CRN safe as agents.

Parameters

Name Type Description Default
n_serotypes int how many serotypes (up to 4) 4
cross float the protection against the other serotypes after recovering from one (1 is complete) 0.3
init_prev float the fraction of people who start infected (infectious) with each serotype 0.001
kwargs dict the parameters of each serotype (see ssl.DengueSerotype), e.g. m=3, and anything else for ss.Disease

Examples

import starsim as ss
import starsim.library as ssl

sim = ss.Sim(diseases=ssl.Dengue(), demographics=True, n_agents=5000, unit='day', stop=365*5)
sim.run()
sim.den1.plot()

Without births, each serotype burns through the people susceptible to it and stops; demographics=True gives the sim births and deaths, which keep the serotypes returning.

DengueSerotype

library.diseases.DengueSerotype(others=(), cross=0.3, **kwargs)

One dengue serotype: people are susceptible, infectious, or recovered, with mosquitoes of its own

The host and vector structure is that of ssl.Malaria, plus the extrinsic incubation period in the mosquito. Having recovered from any of the other serotypes (others=, by name) protects in part (cross), a modifier of this serotype’s rel_sus. ssl.Dengue makes the serotypes together, each protecting against the others.

Note: CRN safe as agents.

Ebola

library.diseases.Ebola(
    *args,
    pars=None,
    strains=None,
    stratify=None,
    solver=None,
    _names=None,
    **kwargs,
)

Ebola, including severe disease and transmission from unburied bodies

People infected are latent (E) for dur_exp, then symptomatic (I). A fraction p_sev become severely ill (Sev), and the rest recover; of the severe, a fraction p_death die, and the rest recover. Severe cases are more infectious (sev_factor), and so are the bodies of those who have died (D), until they are buried (unburied_factor): a fraction p_safe_bury are buried safely, at once, and the rest after dur_dead2buried. Bodies are people until they are buried, when they leave the population; the outcome burial counts both kinds of burial (safe_burial and unsafe_burial), and deaths every death. It is declared, so it runs under any solver.

Sources: the structure follows the model of Legrand J et al., “Understanding the dynamics of Ebola epidemics”, Epidemiol Infect 2007;135(4):610-621 (https://doi.org/10.1017/S0950268806007217), with funeral transmission; the durations and probabilities are illustrative of the 2014 West African epidemic (WHO Ebola Response Team, N Engl J Med 2014;371:1481-1495, https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1411100), and beta gives an R0 of about 1.7, within its estimates of 1.5 to 2.0.

Note: CRN safe as agents.

Parameters

sev_factor float the relative transmissibility of severe cases
unburied_factor float the relative transmissibility of unburied bodies
dur_exp ss.Duration/ss.Dist the time from infection to symptoms (and being infectious); each duration is a lognormal by default, and a single duration given is the mean of an exponential
dur_symp2sev ss.Duration/ss.Dist the time from symptoms to severe disease, for those who become severe
dur_symp2rec ss.Duration/ss.Dist the time from symptoms to recovery, for those who do not
dur_sev2dead ss.Duration/ss.Dist the time from severe disease to death, for those who die
dur_sev2rec ss.Duration/ss.Dist the time from severe disease to recovery, for those who recover
dur_dead2buried ss.Duration/ss.Dist the time from death to an unsafe burial
p_sev float the probability of severe disease
p_death float the probability of death, of the severe
p_safe_bury float the probability of a safe burial, at once
kwargs dict passed to ss.Disease, e.g. init_prev, init or name

Gonorrhea leaves no lasting immunity: people clear the infection, by treatment or on their own, and are susceptible again at once. Nobody is treated here, so the level gonorrhea settles at is above what a setting with treatment would show, and it depends strongly on the network: with few partners and few acts it dies out instead.

HIV

library.diseases.HIV(pars=None, **kwargs)

Simple HIV model with CD4 count dynamics and ART.

Infected agents have a CD4 count that declines towards cd4_min while untreated and recovers towards cd4_max while on ART, at a rate set by cd4_rate. The per-timestep probability of death scales with how far the CD4 count has fallen, so agents with low CD4 counts die soonest. Agents on ART have their transmissibility reduced by art_efficacy. It is written as code, as agents (see ss.AgentDisease), which gives it its seeding, transmission, and the infection and prevalence results; it adds the CD4 count, ART, and death.

Use with ART to scale up treatment over time, and CD4_analyzer to record CD4 counts. Since beta defaults to 0, it must be set for transmission to occur.

The amplitude of the cycle and the duration of immunity are illustrative, and since everyone here becomes fully susceptible again, the epidemics infect more people each year than real seasons do.

Malaria as the Ross-Macdonald model of humans and mosquitoes

People are susceptible, infectious, or recovered, and are born and die; mosquitoes are susceptible or infectious, and stay infectious until they die. Mosquitoes are a count, not people (Sv, Iv), and start at their density of m per person, all susceptible.

Source: the Ross-Macdonald model, in the form of Keeling MJ, Rohani P, Modeling Infectious Diseases in Humans and Animals, Princeton University Press, 2008, section 4.4. Every default here is from its Program 4.4: the biting rate a (its r of 0.5 per 1,000 humans), the transmission probabilities b and c (its transmission matrix), gamma, mu_h, mu_v, and m (its 10,091 mosquitoes per 1,000 people). The model is originally that of Ross R, The Prevention of Malaria, Murray, 1911, and Macdonald G, The Epidemiology and Control of Malaria, Oxford University Press, 1957.

R0() is 14.6 with these values, which is the square root of the classical Ross-Macdonald value, m·a²·b·c/(γ·μ_v) = 213, since each passage through a mosquito and back is two generations.

Note: CRN safe as agents.

MalariaImmunity

library.diseases.MalariaImmunity(
    *args,
    pars=None,
    strains=None,
    stratify=None,
    solver=None,
    _names=None,
    **kwargs,
)

Malaria with immunity, as the Garki model

The states keep the source’s names: x1 (nonimmune and negative), x2 (nonimmune, incubating), y1 (positive and infectious), y2 (positive, not infectious, recovering slowly), y3 (immune positive, recovering fast), x3 (immune and negative), and x4 (immune, incubating). People lose infectivity before they gain a fast recovery rate, and are detected less often once immune (q3). Everything about the mosquitoes is in one number, the vectorial capacity C: the potentially infective contacts each person makes per day through them. Inoculation saturates as C rises (h = g(1 − exp(−C·y1))), and superinfection slows recovery (R(h) = h/(exp(h/r) − 1)).

Source: the Garki model of Dietz K, Molineaux L, Thomas A, “A malaria model tested in the African savannah”, Bull World Health Organ 1974;50(3-4):347-357, in the form given by Molineaux L, Gramiccia G, The Garki Project, WHO, 1980, chapter 10. The defaults are its Table 31: g, alpha2, and r1 were fitted to the Garki data, and alpha1, delta, dur_inc, ratio (r2/r1), and q3 were assumed. C varied by season and village from below 0.01 to above 2 (its Fig. 77), so the default here is a constant chosen for illustration; give an ss.seasonal() for a seasonal one.

Malaria dies out below the critical vectorial capacity, (alpha1 + delta)/g, which is 0.022 per day with these values, as the source gives.

Note: CRN safe as agents.

C float the vectorial capacity: potentially infective contacts per person per day
g float the chance that an infection results, given at least one inoculation
r1 ss.per the recovery rate of nonimmune people, before superinfection
ratio float the recovery rate of immune people, relative to r1
alpha1 ss.per the rate of losing infectivity
alpha2 ss.per the rate of acquiring a high recovery rate (immunity)
delta ss.per the birth and death rate
dur_inc ss.Duration the incubation period in people
q3 float the chance that an immune positive person is detected by a blood examination
kwargs dict passed to ss.Disease, e.g. init or name

Syphilis, as a staged infection, wholly declared

People who are infected incubate (E), then have a primary chancre (P), then secondary syphilis (Se), and then early latent syphilis (El), from which some relapse to secondary syphilis and the rest pass to late latent syphilis (Ll). How infectious a person with latent syphilis is falls with the time since their latent stage began, a quantity each person carries: two modifiers of rel_trans, members of the disease. Since it acts on each person, this disease runs as agents.

Tuberculosis, as the LSHTM model of the spectrum of TB disease

People who are infected either clear the infection, or develop disease that moves back and forth between non-infectious, asymptomatic (infectious, but at trans_asymp of a symptomatic person), and symptomatic. People who cleared or recovered can be infected again, at their own relative rate. Only symptomatic people die of TB; every death is replaced by a birth, so the population stays the same size, as in the source.

Source: the LSHTM natural history of Schwalb A et al., PLOS Glob Public Health 2025, 5(1):e0005050 (https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgph.0005050), in the form of the reference ODE of TBsim (tbsim/compartmental/lshtm_ode.R), from which every default here is taken: beta, trans_asymp, the reinfection multipliers, the rates between states, and the background death rate. Treatment is off in that reference, and is left out here; TBsim adds treatment, drug resistance, and preventive therapy.

With the reference’s beta of 9, TB declines slowly over centuries (R0() is 0.23, since most infections are cleared and few reach disease), reproducing that reference exactly; beta is what a model of a particular place fits to its data.

Rates are per year, so a step of a year or a month suits it.
