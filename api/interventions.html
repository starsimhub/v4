interventions

interventions

Interventions, which change the course of a sim during a run

Interventions typically include things like testing, treatment, and vaccination. A delivery (ss.Delivery, and ss.vaccinate(), ss.screen(), and ss.treat()) gives a product to the people chosen, and exists so that the same intervention can be written once and run under any solver. It also works with a disease written as code, as agents.

Used with: agents and compartments; declared and coded modules

Deliver a product, or move people along a stratification; see ss.vaccinate(), ss.screen(), and ss.treat()

Who receives it is who=: a condition on people (who is eligible), or an event (the people it happened to); where= is anything else it depends on, including an event, and when= a condition on time. With an event in who= or where=, delivery is triggered: it reaches the people the event happened to on this step who meet the conditions. Otherwise, with at or interval, delivery happens in rounds, and in each round every eligible person is offered the product once; and with neither, delivery is routine: each person is offered the product once, at the first step on which they are eligible.

A delivery is an event too (the people it reached on the step), and so is each outcome of it (a test’s positive and negative, a cure’s cured, failed, and unneeded): a follow-up is a step of its own, triggered by one (ss.treat(drug, who=screening.positive)), and runs after it in the same step. Each event remembers per person whether (.ever), how often (.count), and how long ago (.since) it happened, including on this step once the delivery has run. With no product and no move, a delivery is a step people attend (e.g. a clinic visit): its event is who attended, which several steps can take. delivery.reached() is the UIDs of the people it reached on the step (agents only).

Its methods are how it runs as agents; in a compartmental run, what differs is delivery.in_compartments (see ss.solvers.CompartmentalDelivery). A product whose effect is code of your own is an ss.Product with an administer(uids) method.

Note: CRN safe as agents.

Examples

# Seeding: three people, chosen from everyone susceptible, infected every 30 days for ten years
seeds = ss.Delivery(move=S >> I, capacity=3, start=ss.days(30), interval=ss.days(30), stop=ss.years(10))

# A move along a stratification for 70% of people in two rounds, e.g. a campaign that leaves a mark
rollout = ss.Delivery(move=dict(vaccinated=True), coverage=0.7, at=[5, 30], who=~sir.vaccinated)

# Each round of a campaign adds a dose (with sim.stratify(doses=[0, 1, 2, 3]))
campaign = ss.Delivery(increment='doses', coverage=0.6, at=[2025, 2026, 2027])

# A test, and a treatment of those who test positive, as two steps
screening = ss.screen(sensitivity=0.9, specificity=0.95, who=ss.ppl.age > 30, coverage=0.4, interval=7)
treatment = ss.treat(cure=0.95, who=screening.positive, coverage=0.8)

age_reason
interventions.Delivery.age_reason(sim)

With at_age=: why a compartmental run cannot deliver it, or None. As compartments, people reach the age by aging from the age group below it into the one it starts, so the sim needs age groups with an edge at the age. It is an error rather than a reason for solver='auto' to run as agents (see ss.solvers.init_capabilities()), since a comparison with a baseline without it would then compare two solvers.

Intervention

interventions.Intervention(*args, eligibility=None, **kwargs)

Base class for interventions.

Deliver a diagnostic test; see ss.Delivery for the arguments

delay= is the step’s, the time from its trigger to the test, as for any step; the time from the test to its result is the test’s (ss.Diagnostic(delay=...)).

Its outcomes, positive and negative, are events on the step the result is available (after the test’s delay), and results: the number with each result on each step. What happens next is a step of its own, triggered by an outcome.

Note: CRN safe as agents.

Examples

S, I, R = ss.states(3)
sir = ss.Disease(infection=S >> I @ 0.3*I, recovery=ss.Transition(I, R, dur=10), init_prev=0.01)
screening = ss.screen(sensitivity=0.9, specificity=0.98, coverage=0.4, interval=7)
treatment = ss.treat(cure=0.95, who=screening.positive, coverage=0.8)
sim = ss.Sim(sir, interventions=[screening, treatment], stop=100).run()
