interventions

interventions

Interventions, which change the course of a sim during a run

Interventions typically include things like testing, treatment, and vaccination. An event (ss.Event) moves the people it reaches, on a schedule or when another event happens, e.g. seeding infections; a delivery (ss.Delivery, and ss.vaccinate(), ss.screen(), and ss.treat()) is an event that gives a product. Both exist so that the same intervention can be written once and run under any solver. It also works with a disease written as code, as agents.

Used with: agents and compartments; declared and coded modules

Deliver a product to people: an ss.Event with what only interventions need; see ss.vaccinate(), ss.screen(), and ss.treat()

Who and when are as for ss.Event. What is delivered is a product (an ss.Vaccine, ss.Diagnostic, or ss.Treatment, or one written as code, an ss.Product with an administer(uids) method), or a move, as for an event, which a campaign can make (e.g. dict(vaccinated=True)). How many is said as for services: coverage=, the fraction reached, and capacity=, how many can be served, with ss.capacity() for who is served first and whether the rest wait. With an event as when=, the delivery reaches the people it happened to: an outcome of another delivery (when=screening.positive) on the same step, a transition of a disease (when=tb.care) on the next, since deliveries run before the diseases in each step.

A delivery is an event too, and so is each outcome of its product (a test’s positive and negative, a cure’s cured, failed, and unneeded): a follow-up is a delivery of its own, triggered by one (ss.treat(drug, when=screening.positive)), and runs after it in the same step.

Its methods are how it runs as agents; in a compartmental run, what differs is delivery.in_compartments (see ss.solvers.CompartmentalDelivery).

Note: CRN safe as agents.

Examples

# A campaign that leaves a mark, for 70% of the unvaccinated in two rounds
rollout = ss.Delivery(dict(vaccinated=True), coverage=0.7, when=[5, 30], who=~sir.vaccinated)

# A test, and a treatment of those who test positive, as two deliveries
screening = ss.screen(sensitivity=0.9, specificity=0.95, who=ss.person.age > 30, coverage=0.4, when=ss.schedule(every=ss.days(7)))
treatment = ss.treat(cure=0.95, when=screening.positive, coverage=0.8)

# Routine vaccination at nine months, from 2025 (the same as when=ss.schedule(start=2025))
mcv1 = ss.vaccinate(efficacy=0.85, who=ss.at_age(ss.months(9)), coverage=0.8, start=2025)

# Testing those with symptoms, from 2001 to 2010
testing = ss.screen(sensitivity=0.9, when=sir.infection, coverage=0.3, start=2001, stop=2010)

Event

interventions.Event(
    what=None,
    who=None,
    when=None,
    n=None,
    p=None,
    strain=None,
    increment=None,
    start=None,
    stop=None,
    name=None,
    label=None,
    **kwargs,
)

Something that happens to people: a move between states, on a schedule or when another event happens; see ss.Delivery to deliver a product

An event has three parts, each optional. What happens to those it reaches: a move between two states (S >> I), levels of a stratification (dict(risk=('low', 'high'))), or strains (DS >> RIF), or an increment of a counter; with none, it is only an event, which others trigger on (e.g. a clinic visit). Who it reaches: a condition on people, or on the population (sir.I.n > 1000, which decides for everyone at once), or people as they reach an age (ss.at_age()). And when: at times (when=[2025, 2030], when='2024-03-01', or when=ss.days(30), 30 days after the start), on a schedule (when=ss.schedule(every=ss.months(1), start='2024-01-01', stop='2025-01-01')), when another event happens (when=sir.infection, or when=ss.on(sir.infection, delay=ss.days(5))), or, with no when=, routinely: each person once, at the first step they are eligible. start= and stop= limit any of these to a window (the same as when=ss.schedule(start=..., stop=...) for routine events), e.g. when=screening.positive, start=2001.

n= and p= say how many it reaches each time, as for ss.move(): at most n of those eligible, chosen at random, and each with probability p.

It is an event itself: others trigger on it (when=seeding), and it remembers per person whether (.ever), how often (.n_times), and how long ago (.since) it happened. event.reached() is the UIDs of the people it reached on the step (agents only).

Note: CRN safe as agents.

Examples

# Seeding: three people, chosen from everyone susceptible, infected every 30 days for ten years
seeds = ss.Event(S >> I, n=3, when=ss.schedule(every=ss.days(30), start=ss.days(30), stop=ss.years(10)))

# A new strain on a date
alpha = ss.Event(S >> E, n=20, when='2021-01-01', strain='alpha')

# Twenty importations a year, for the first five years
imports = ss.Event(S >> I, n=ss.peryear(20), stop=ss.years(5))

# Moving people to high risk, five days after infection, half of them
risky = ss.Event(dict(risk=('low', 'high')), when=ss.on(sir.infection, delay=ss.days(5)), p=0.5)

# Each round of a campaign adds a dose (with sim.stratify(doses=[0, 1, 2, 3]))
campaign = ss.Event(increment='doses', p=0.6, when=[2025, 2026, 2027])

age_reason
interventions.Event.age_reason(sim)

With at_age=: why a compartmental run cannot deliver it, or None. As compartments, people reach the age by aging from the age group below it into the one it starts, so the sim needs age groups with an edge at the age. It is an error rather than a reason for solver='auto' to run as agents (see ss.solvers.init_capabilities()), since a comparison with a baseline without it would then compare two solvers.

Intervention

interventions.Intervention(*args, who=None, **kwargs)

Base class for interventions written as code

Write one when the built-in events and deliveries cannot say what the intervention does. Its key method is step(), called on each timestep, which can change the states of the diseases (e.g. treating or vaccinating agents). To deliver a product, or to move people between states, use ss.vaccinate(), ss.screen(), ss.treat(), or ss.Event() instead, which run under every solver.

An intervention written as code can be given who=, who it can reach, as for a delivery: a condition on people (ss.person.age < 5), or a function of the sim (and of the intervention, as self) returning UIDs or a mask. Its step() then reads the people eligible now with check_eligibility().

Parameters

Name Type Description Default
args list passed to ss.Module
who Cond / func who it can reach (by default everyone alive), read by check_eligibility() None
kwargs dict passed to ss.Module

Example:

class Discount(ss.Intervention):
    def step(self):
        uids = self.check_eligibility() # Those who= holds for now, alive
        self.sim.diseases.sis.rel_trans[uids] = 0.5

sim = ss.Sim(diseases='sis', networks='random', interventions=Discount(who=ss.person.age < 15)).run()

at_age

interventions.at_age(age, who=None)

Who an event reaches: people as they reach an age, as its who=; e.g. ss.vaccinate(who=ss.at_age(ss.months(9)))

Each person is reached once, at the first step on which they are that age or older; not those already past it when the event starts, nor (as agents) those who arrive past it later, e.g. immigrants. As compartments, people reach it by aging into the age group it starts, so the sim needs age groups with an edge at the age (an error otherwise). Join it to other conditions with &, e.g. ss.at_age(ss.years(9)) & ss.person.female.

Parameters

Name Type Description Default
age dur / float the age (a bare number is in years) required
who Cond any other condition on who is reached; usually given by joining with & instead None

capacity

interventions.capacity(n, priority=None, queue=False)

How many a delivery can serve, who is served first, and what happens to the rest, as its capacity=

capacity=n is the same as capacity=ss.capacity(n): this is for when there is a priority or a queue too.

Parameters

Name Type Description Default
n float / rate the number that can be served: per round (or per step, for delivery triggered by an event or by a function), or a rate (e.g. ss.peryear(50_000)), which routine delivery needs, since it has no rounds for a number to be per; a rate in rounds gives what accrues between them required
priority Cond / list / Expr / func who is served first: a condition, or a list of them served in order (all of the first, then the second); a score, highest first: an expression (e.g. -hiv.cd4, the lowest CD4 first) or a function of the sim (or of (sim, uids), agents only); or a list of conditions and scores, each breaking the ties of the ones before (e.g. [due, ss.person.age], those due first, and among them the oldest); other ties are broken at random None
queue bool / dur what happens to those not served (agents only): False (the default), they are lost; True, they wait until served; a duration, they wait at most that long, and those still waiting then are the outcome never_ran. Those waiting are served before anyone newly reached in their class (longest first), and leave the queue if they stop being eligible. A queue is for a service with a waiting list (e.g. treatment slots), where demand turned away comes back rather than being lost False

Examples

art = ss.treat(drug, who=hiv.diagnosed, capacity=ss.capacity(ss.peryear(50_000), priority=-hiv.cd4, queue=True))

Deliver a diagnostic test; see ss.Delivery for the arguments

when=ss.on(event, delay=...) is the delivery’s, the time from its trigger to the test, as for any delivery; the time from the test to its result is the test’s (ss.Diagnostic(result_delay=...)).

Its outcomes, positive and negative, are events on the step the result is available (after the test’s result_delay), and results: the number with each result on each step. What happens next is a step of its own, triggered by an outcome (when=screening.positive).

Note: CRN safe as agents.

Examples

S, I, R = ss.states(3)
sir = ss.Disease(infection=S >> I @ 0.3*I, recovery=ss.Transition(I, R, dur=10), init_prev=0.01)
screening = ss.screen(sensitivity=0.9, specificity=0.98, coverage=0.4, when=ss.schedule(every=ss.days(7)))
treatment = ss.treat(cure=0.95, when=screening.positive, coverage=0.8)
sim = ss.Sim(sir, interventions=[screening, treatment], stop=100).run()
