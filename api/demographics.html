demographics

demographics

Births, deaths, migration, and pregnancy

These exist so that the population can change during a run. ss.Births and ss.Deaths are written as code, ss.birth() and the like declare the same flows for the whole population, and ss.Pregnancy is declared, so that conception and delivery are transitions that other modules can modify or respond to.

Used with: agents and compartments; declared and coded modules

Births at a crude birth rate, without modeling pregnancy: for when the number of births matters but not the pregnancies behind them (for those, use ss.Pregnancy)

The birth rate can vary by year. The number of births in each step is the birth rate times the number of people alive; each newborn’s parent (people.parent) is a woman of reproductive age (min_age to max_age).

Note: CRN safe.

Deaths

demographics.Deaths(
    pars=None,
    rel_death=_,
    death_rate=_,
    rate_per=_,
    columns=None,
    lifetable=None,
    **kwargs,

Background deaths: deaths from causes other than the diseases modeled

Each agent’s chance of dying in each step comes from death_rate and the length of the step. By default, this is ss.peryear(0.01) (10 per 1,000 people per year), so everyone faces about a 1% chance of death per year. For more realism, give rates that vary by year, sex, and age (a table, a life table, or a place by name, for its UN rates); columns= says which columns of a table are which.

Note: CRN safe.

Demographics

demographics.Demographics(name=None, label=None, **kwargs)

Base class for demographic modules.

A demographic module typically handles births/deaths/migration and takes place at the start of the timestep, before networks are updated and before any disease modules are executed. See ss.Births, ss.Deaths, and ss.Pregnancy for built-in implementations. Demographic modules modify the ss.People population and can interact with ss.Network objects (e.g. to add newborns to contact networks).

Flows

demographics.Flows(transitions, **kwargs)

Births, deaths, and migration declared for the whole population, as ss.Sim(demographics=[ss.birth(...), ss.death(...)]) gives them

Every declared disease’s people are born and die by them, once, under every solver: they become transitions of the first declared disease (or the first with the state that births name), whose people are everyone’s, since as agents the diseases share the people, and in a compartmental run with several declared diseases, their states are tracked jointly (see ss.solvers.choose.init_flows()). With no declared disease, the sim adds one of everyone alive to hold them (ss.SimPars.population()), so they are a declared model like any other. This class is not usually used directly.

Note: CRN safe as agents.

Pregnancy

demographics.Pregnancy(pars=None, fetal_agents='auto', columns=None, **kwargs)

Pregnancy, as declared states and transitions

Everyone is in NP (not pregnant: men and children too) or P (pregnant). Gestational age is P.time_in. Conception is one transition, so contraception, HIV, or LAM are modifiers of it (ss.modify(preg.conception, multiply=...)). A pregnancy ends in an abortion (at once, with p_abortion), a miscarriage or stillbirth (a hazard before or after loss_threshold), a maternal death (antepartum, with maternal_death_rate), or a delivery at the end of a dwell of dur_pregnancy. Each delivery gives children children (birth): as agents, new agents whose parent is the mother, placed in each declared module’s state given by its own ss.birth(when=preg.delivery) (or else its first state). Children die before the age of 1 at infant_death_rate (infant_mortality; 0 by default). It runs as agents under solver='auto'; a compartmental solver named explicitly runs a small approximate form (see compartmental_form()).

For other modules, preg.delivery happens to the mother, and to her fetuses (if they are agents: see _init_fetus()) before they are born, so a death of a fetus when=preg.delivery is a stillbirth; preg.birth happens to the newborns, so a neonatal death is when=preg.birth. Code reads a step’s newborns and their mothers in that step, before the diseases’ step, with on_birth(parents, children) in any module (see ss.Module.on_birth()), which is called at the delivery; the mothers delivered in the step are also preg.ti_delivered == sim.ti.

As agents, a live birth starts breastfeeding (lactating, with p_breastfeed), or restarts it for a woman breastfeeding already; it ends after dur_breastfeed, or at once if a child of hers dies before the age of 1. It is a state of each woman rather than one of the module’s exclusive states (NP, P), since it overlaps the next pregnancy. A BreastfeedingNet pairs each newborn with a mother who breastfeeds, and ends the pair when she stops.

As agents, a woman who leaves P (a delivery, a loss, or an abortion) faces conception again in the same step, for what is left of it (half a step); the children and records of a delivery (ti_delivered, since_birth, lactating) are made first, so a rate or a modifier that reads them sees this delivery. A rate given as a function (e.g. an override of conception_rate()) can be called more than once a step, for those who arrive in NP within it, so it should not draw random numbers.

Its parameters are listed in __init__(); code reacts to its events with add_callback(), e.g. preg.add_callback(preg.birth, fn). Its states and results are P (pregnant), ti_P, the scheduled delivery ti_exit_P (set_delivery() reschedules it), and the flows conception, birth, miscarriage, stillbirth, and abortion; as agents, lactating (breastfeeding).

Note: CRN safe as agents.

# Nigeria's age-specific fertility rates, from the UN

# Lactational amenorrhea: fewer conceptions while breastfeeding, in the first 6 months

burnin_plan
demographics.Pregnancy.burnin_plan(engine)

burnin=True (the default): women pregnant at the start. Each woman who could conceive has the chance of having conceived in the mean duration of a pregnancy before the start, at her conception hazard then (1 - exp(-h*dur)), at a step uniform over it, and is run forward by the pregnancy’s own transitions (abortion, losses, and delivery, with no births), so the start has the gestational ages and the first steps their deliveries. In the compartmental form, the same fraction of each group, less the abortions and the losses on the way, is spread evenly over gestation (see ss.solvers.Compartmental.backfill_burnin()).

compartmental_form
demographics.Pregnancy.compartmental_form(sim)

The small compartmental form, used only when a compartmental solver is named (solver='auto' runs a pregnancy as agents): the states NP and P, conception, abortion, maternal deaths, and the delivery at the end of dur_pregnancy (a fixed delay, or Erlang stages for a distribution), with n children per delivery (ss.birth(when=preg.delivery)). The losses do not read the gestational age (P.time_in): the loss rate is split between miscarriage and stillbirth by the fraction of the mean gestation below loss_threshold. It has no fetuses, no links between mothers and children, no breastfeeding, no preterm risk, and no deaths at delivery (p_maternal_death). Returns the note that says so

preterm_risk
demographics.Pregnancy.preterm_risk(uids, rel=None)

At conception: each woman’s relative risk of preterm birth (rel_preterm), her baseline (rel_preterm) times the risk at her age (preterm.by_age), multiplies her probability of a preterm duration (below preterm.threshold), capped at 1; the durations within the preterm and the term ones keep their fractions. For dur_pregnancy as an ss.choice (the default), each duration is drawn so (one uniform number, by inverse transform), kept in weeks (dur_gestation, the gestational age at birth), and the delivery scheduled by it, as the engine schedules a dwell time (agents only)

Called again during a pregnancy, it draws the duration again: with rel, a relative risk for this draw on top of hers (e.g. an infection acquired while pregnant), which her baseline for later pregnancies does not keep. A delivery it would schedule in a step already past is taken in the next step (see set_delivery()).

preterm

demographics.preterm(threshold=None, very_threshold=None, rel=None, by_age=None)

The risk of preterm birth in ss.Pregnancy (preterm=ss.preterm(...)), for the options that are rarely changed

At conception, each woman’s relative risk of preterm birth (rel, drawn for her once, times the risk at her age, by_age) multiplies the probability of a duration of pregnancy below threshold (see ss.Pregnancy.preterm_risk()). Newborns below threshold are preterm (preg.preterm), and below very_threshold very preterm.

Parameters

Name Type Description Default
threshold dur the gestational age below which a birth is preterm (default 37 weeks) None
very_threshold dur the gestational age below which a birth is very preterm (default 32 weeks) None
rel float / Dist each woman’s baseline relative risk, from factors other than her age (default ss.normal(mean=1, std=0.1)) None
by_age array the relative risk by the mother’s age: the upper edge of each age group, and the risk in it (default [[18, 35, 1000], [1.2, 1, 1.2]]: 1.2 below 18 and from 35) None

Example

preg = ss.Pregnancy(preterm=ss.preterm(rel=ss.constant(1), by_age=[[18, 35, 1000], [1.5, 1, 1.3]]))

bare_rate

demographics.bare_rate(rate, name)

A crude rate of births or deaths of everyone (ss.birth(), ss.death()), read as ss.Births and ss.Deaths read theirs: a rate above 1 per year, given as a bare number or typed, is per 1,000 people (e.g. 30, or ss.peryear(30)), and one of 1 or below is per person (e.g. 0.03, or ss.peryear(0.03); see rate_per_of()). A bare number is a rate per year. Anything else (e.g. a function) is returned as it is.

Returns the rate, per person, and a note saying how it was read (None for a typed rate per person), which is said with say_bare() once the module is in a sim: these are the usual ways to give these rates, so it is not a warning

A time series is read the same way, from the median of its values.

Parameters

Name Type Description Default
rate any the rate as given required
name str what it is, for the note (e.g. “rate”) required

Examples

rate, note = ss.demographics.bare_rate(30, 'rate') # ss.peryear(0.03): 30 per 1,000 people per year
rate, note = ss.demographics.bare_rate(ss.peryear(30), 'rate') # The same
rate, note = ss.demographics.bare_rate(ss.timeseries({2000: 40, 2030: 20}), 'rate') # ss.peryear(0.04) falling to ss.peryear(0.02)

period_year

demographics.period_year(years, year)

The year of the data that applies at year: the latest at or before it, as ss.timeseries holds each value until the next

Rates labeled by the start of their period (e.g. the UN’s “2010” for 2010 to 2014) apply from that year until the next label. Before the first label, the first applies.

rate_per_of

demographics.rate_per_of(per, rates, name)

How many people the rates of ss.Births, ss.Deaths, or ss.Pregnancy are per: rate_per as given, or else read from their size. Rates of births, deaths, and conceptions are well below 1 per person per year, and well above 1 per 1,000, so rates above 1 per year (the median, for a table) are per 1,000 people, as data usually are (e.g. ss.Births(birth_rate=20), or ss.peryear(20)), and rates of 1 or below are per person (e.g. ss.peryear(0.02), or the UN’s death rates by age, mx). This is the same for a bare number, a typed rate, and a table. rate_per=1000 or rate_per=1 says which.

Parameters

Name Type Description Default
per float rate_per as given: None to read it from the rates, 1000 for per 1,000, 1 for per person required
rates number / rate / timeseries / dataframe the rates, or rate data required
name str what the rates are, for the error (e.g. “birth_rate”) required
