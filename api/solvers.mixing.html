solvers.mixing

solvers.mixing

Not for the user: who meets whom in a compartmental run: the contact rates between groups that the networks become

It exists so that the networks of a model, usually written as code, can run without agents. A run as agents uses it too, for the contact matrices of mixing pools and the movement between places.

Used with: agents and compartments; declared and coded modules

Mixing Who meets whom in a compartmental run

Mixing

solvers.mixing.Mixing(sim)

Who meets whom in a compartmental run

With no networks, mixing is homogeneous with one contact per unit time, so exposure to a state is the fraction of the population in it. A mixing pool sets the contact rate; mixing pools between age groups give a contact matrix; an individual-level network is approximated by its mean number of contacts, and lasting partnerships by the partner-change model (see partnerships()).

count_terms
solvers.mixing.Mixing.count_terms(terms=None)

The contact matrices that act on a count in a rate (I.n, I.frac: see ss.graph.Graph.met_counts), of the terms given or the sim’s; with none, a count is the number in the same place

A matrix acts on what the rate says: beta*I is exposure to the fraction of each group (beta·Σ_j C_ij·I_j/N_j), beta*I.n to the number in each (beta·Σ_j C_ij·I_j), and beta*I.frac to that number over everyone in the same place (beta·Σ_j C_ij·I_j/N). Other networks do not act on a count, as without a matrix.

edge_dur
solvers.mixing.Mixing.edge_dur(net, sim)

How long each edge of a network transmits over before it is drawn again, in units of the sim’s time, for its approximation by homogeneous mixing: the network’s own step (a disease that steps less often uses its own: see ss.solvers.Compartmental.saturation()); None for a network whose edges never change (dynamic=False)

As agents, an edge transmits at most once to its susceptible end over a step D, with probability 1 − e^(−b·D) for a rate b, so the force of infection over K contacts per unit time is K·(1 − e^(−b·D))/D, not the K·b of mass action. The two agree as K → ∞ with K·b held fixed, but on a sparse network with long steps the difference matters, most of all near the epidemic threshold. Edges that last several steps (ss.RandomNet(dur=)) are taken a step at a time: within a lasting edge, the partners’ states change (recovery, reinfection), which is the partner-change model’s concern (ss.DegreeNet).

init_pools
solvers.mixing.Mixing.init_pools()

As agents: the contact matrices of mixing pools from counts in each group, in a model with places (within each place), and for a matrix over the model’s own dimensions (ss.contacts()), which is then exact

A mixing pool’s hazard is worked out from the fraction of each group that is infectious, so the contact matrix is used as in a compartmental run, from the agents’ counts in each group (see ss.solvers.Agents.transmit()), with any movement between places. Over the model’s dimensions, everyone in a group has the same contacts, so this is what a pool for each pair of groups would give, without making one.

partnerships
solvers.mixing.Mixing.partnerships(net)

Lasting partnerships (ss.DegreeNet, ss.SexualNet) under the partner-change model: a matrix of the partnerships formed per unit time by one person in each cell (a side, by a level of a dimension) with people in each other, φ_i·m_ij, with φ_i = n_i/D̄ (partners at a time over how long partnerships last), split by the side of the partner, since the chance a partnership transmits differs by direction (see Compartmental.partnership_chance())

The sides of the network and the property of people it matches or counts partners by must be dimensions of the model. Partners are drawn from the population at random, in proportion to the partners each person has, within the other side for a network between men and women, and with match=, the fraction at the same level that it gives.

within_places
solvers.mixing.Mixing.within_places(term)

Make a contact matrix act within each place

The cells of the matrix become pairs of a place and a level, and the movement between places applies to each level: people mix by the matrix with the people present where they are. A matrix that is given over the places themselves already says who meets whom across them, so it is used as it is.

The mixing is the product of the time fraction between places and the matrix between levels (np.kron(frac, matrix)), but is kept as the two factors, which is much smaller and faster with many places: see term_exposure().
