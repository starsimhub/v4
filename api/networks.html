networks

networks

Networks and mixing pools: who meets whom, and so who can infect whom

A network holds the contacts (edges) between people, over which a disease spreads. Mixing pools (ss.MixingPool, ss.MixingPools) and contact matrices (ss.contacts()) spread it between groups instead, without edges. These exist so that a model’s contacts are separate from its diseases, and one network can carry several diseases. In a compartmental run, a network is replaced by what it implies: contact rates, a matrix, or a model of partner change.

Used with: agents and compartments; declared and coded modules

DegreeNet

networks.DegreeNet(
    pars=None,
    n_contacts=_,
    dur=_,
    concurrency=_,
    max_contacts=_,
    match=_,
    acts=_,
    where=_,
    between=_,
    **kwargs,
)

Partnerships that form and dissolve, with a target number of partners at a time

The number of partners people have at any moment (n_contacts) is a target for the whole population, not a fixed number for each person: partnerships end at random, and people look for new ones at a rate that depends on how many they already have, chosen so that the distribution of the number of partners stays at the target. Everyone moves through it over time, as in EpiModel’s networks fitted to target statistics: this covers the same ground as a dyad-independent exponential random graph model (ERGM), with the terms for edges, degree, and mixing, but not terms such as triangles. The network starts at the target. In an open population, partnerships that end at a constant rate (dur a mean) end less often by as many as are ended by a partner leaving (dying), as EpiModel’s d.rate correction does, so they last as long as given on average; with any other distribution of durations, people look for partners more (or less) often, by a factor that keeps the mean number of partners at the target. Partnerships are not only sexual: people who share needles have partners too. For sexual partnerships, with an age of debut, see ss.SexualNet.

Each partnership can be between one person of each of two groups (between), e.g. a man and a woman, so a disease’s beta on it can differ by direction; each group can then have its own number of partners (n_contacts as a dict). With no target (n_contacts=None), people who have no partner are paired on every step, so nobody has more than one at a time.

A subclass that overrides step() forms its own partnerships: the base starts none for it, and has none of the parameters for forming them, only between.

Note: not CRN safe when people are born or die.

Parameters

Name Type Description Default
n_contacts float/ss.Dist/ss.by/dict the number of partners a person has at a time: a distribution over whole numbers (e.g. ss.poisson(0.7)), or a number, which is the mean of a Poisson; or a number for each level of a property of people (ss.by(risk=[0.4, 1.6])), as EpiModel’s nodefactor; or one for each group of between, e.g. dict(fsw=3, client=0.5) (each partnership has one of each, so these should give the two groups about as many partners in all); or None, for at most one partner, pairing people who have none on every step _
dur float/ss.dur/ss.Dist how long partnerships last: a mean (they end at a constant rate), or a distribution; a bare number is in the sim’s unit _
acts rate/ss.Dist if given, the number of acts per unit time in each partnership: a rate such as ss.freqperweek(2), or a distribution of rates drawn once for each partnership; a disease’s beta is then per act (see ss.peract) _
where Cond / str / func if given, who takes part: a condition over people (e.g. ss.ppl.pwid), as for ss.modify(); nobody else is in the network _
between str / tuple if given, who partnerships are between: 'mf', a man and a woman, so its directions are m2f and f2m and a disease’s beta on it is ss.peract(m2f=..., f2m=...), or one value for both; 'mm', two men, with one beta; or a pair of groups, each the name of a boolean property of people or of a level of a stratification, e.g. ('fsw', 'client'), whose directions are fsw2client and client2fsw (nobody can be in both) _
concurrency float if given, the share of people with two or more partners at once; the distribution of n_contacts is adjusted to give it, keeping its mean _
max_contacts int if given, nobody has more partners than this at once _
match dict if given, the share of partnerships between people at the same level of a property of people (a stratification, or an attribute such as 'female'), e.g. dict(risk=0.8), as EpiModel’s nodematch; or of each of several, e.g. dict(risk=0.8, region=0.6) _
pars dict the same parameters, as a dict None

The realized network is recorded in the results (mean_degree, concurrency, and match, or with several properties match_<name> for each), and net.check() compares it with the targets.

Examples

# EpiModel's introductory network: 175 partnerships among 500 people, 110 people with two or more partners, nobody with four or more, and partnerships lasting 50 steps
net = ss.DegreeNet(n_contacts=0.7, concurrency=0.22, max_contacts=3, dur=50)

# Most partnerships within a risk group, and over half within a region
net = ss.DegreeNet(n_contacts=0.7, dur=50, match=dict(risk=0.8, region=0.6))

# And four times as many partners in the high-risk group (with sim.stratify(risk=['low', 'high']))
net = ss.DegreeNet(n_contacts=ss.by(risk=[0.4, 1.6]), dur=50, match=dict(risk=0.8))

# Needle sharing among people who inject drugs (a boolean property of people), with 20 injections a month in each partnership
needles = ss.DegreeNet(name='needles', where=ss.ppl.pwid, n_contacts=2, dur=ss.months(6), acts=ss.freqpermonth(20))

# Sex work, between female sex workers and their clients (boolean properties of people)
sw = ss.DegreeNet(name='sw', between=('fsw', 'client'), n_contacts=dict(fsw=5, client=0.5), dur=ss.months(1), acts=ss.freqperweek(1))

Pair people who are looking for partners, who are in a random order: in turn, one of each group of between (or two of one), or, with match=, some within their own level, so that the share of partnerships at the same level is the target

Examples

import starsim as ss

# Set the parameters
mp_pars = dict(
    src = lambda sim: sim.people.male, # only males are infectious
    dst = None, # all agents are susceptible
    beta = 0.2,
    n_contacts = ss.poisson(lam=4),
)

# Seed 5% of the male population
def p_init(self, sim, uids):
    return 0.05*sim.people.male

# Create and run the sim
sis = ss.SIS(init_prev=p_init)
mp = ss.MixingPool(**mp_pars)
sim = ss.Sim(diseases=sis, networks=mp)
sim.run()
sim.plot()

A container for creating a rectangular array of MixingPool instances

import starsim as ss

    diseases = 'sis',
    beta = 0.1,
    src = {'0-15': ss.AgeGroup(0, 15), '15+': ss.AgeGroup(15, None)},
    dst = {'0-15': ss.AgeGroup(0, 15), '15+': ss.AgeGroup(15, None)},

)

Network

networks.Network(pars=None, edges=None, **kwargs)

A class holding a single network of contact edges (connections) between people as well as methods for updating these. Networks mediate disease transmission between agents in ss.People; see ss.Disease for how transmission uses network edges, and ss.RandomNet, ss.SexualNet, ss.PrenatalNet for built-in network types.

The input is typically arrays including: person 1 of the connection, person 2 of the connection, the weight of the connection, the duration and start/end times of the connection.

Note: CRN safe, with the edges given.

Parameters

Name Type Description Default
pars dict parameters, for subclasses that define them None
edges dict / dataframe the edges of the network, as an alternative to passing them as keyword arguments None
p1 array an array of length N, the number of connections in the network, with the indices of people on one side of the connection. required
p2 array an array of length N, the number of connections in the network, with the indices of people on the other side of the connection. required
beta array an array representing relative transmissibility of each connection for this network - TODO, do we need this? required
kwargs dict standard module arguments (e.g. name, label, dt) are passed to update_pars(); other keys are copied directly into the network as edges {}

The edges that transmit in a step, in each direction the network transmits in (p1 to p2, then p2 to p1 unless it is one way)

An edge from src to trg transmits with probability rel_trans[src]·rel_sus[trg]·beta, or prob() if it is given. Random numbers are drawn only for the edges that can transmit: with CRN, each edge’s number depends only on its source and target, so this gives the same result as drawing one for every edge. The beta is worked out only for the edges from someone infectious to someone susceptible, which are found for both directions in one pass.

PostnatalNet

networks.PostnatalNet(pars=None, dur=None, **kwargs)

Network to track postnatal processes

This network automatically has edges added between mothers and infants upon birth if a Pregnancy module is present in the simulation. The edges persist until the specified duration has elapsed, at which point they are automatically removed. This allows different PostnatalNet instances have different postnatal periods specific to what is being modeled. Transmission along this network is possible if a beta value >0 is specified. Otherwise, transmission will be skipped.

By default, the postnatal duration is based on the maternal UID and would be the same for all infants of that mother, although derived classes could modify this behavior. It transmits one way only, from the mother (p1) to the child (p2), so its one direction is mother2child.

RandomNet

networks.RandomNet(
    pars=None,
    n_contacts=_,
    dur=_,
    beta=_,
    age_range=_,
    dynamic=_,
    method=_,
    remove_self_loops=_,
    **kwargs,
)

Random connectivity between agents

Each agent has a number of contacts (n_contacts), and the other end of each is drawn from the other agents’ contacts (method='fast', the default), so an agent’s chance of being picked is proportional to its own number of contacts. This keeps the mixing structure of risk-stratified networks (a small high-risk core with many contacts forms most of its edges with itself).

method says how the other end of each contact is drawn:

  • 'fast' (the default): by sampling the contacts with replacement. The source side of each edge is exactly n_contacts, while the target side is Poisson-distributed with mean n_contacts, so agents have somewhat more variable numbers of contacts than with 'exact'; about 1.8x faster than 'exact'.
  • 'exact': by shuffling the contacts (sampling without replacement), so each agent has exactly n_contacts contacts.
  • 'uniform': uniformly from all eligible agents, regardless of their own n_contacts, as in Covasim v3’s random networks. This gives less variable numbers of contacts (e.g. with n_contacts=ss.poisson(20), a variance of about 15, rather than 30 for 'fast' or 20 for 'exact'), but loses the mixing structure above.
  • 'safe': by giving each contact a random place on a line, from the agent’s own random numbers, and joining it to its nearest neighbor there. Adding an agent then changes only the contacts next to theirs, which suits scenario comparisons, but it is slower, and does not yet work within places.

Note: not CRN safe when people are born or die, unless dynamic=False: with any of these methods but 'safe', one death rewires almost every edge.

networks.SexualNet(
    pars=None,

Sexual partnerships that form and dissolve, between two groups of people: by default, men and women

A ss.DegreeNet between men and women (between='mf'), in which people take part after their debut (debut), if they do (participation, drawn at their debut). By default, people who have no partner are paired on every step, so nobody has more than one at a time. With n_contacts, the number of partners people have at a time is a target instead (among people who take part, after their debut), as for ss.DegreeNet; with partnerships much longer than people’s time in the network, it takes a few decades to settle.

Each partnership is between one person of each of two groups (between): a man and a woman, two men, or e.g. a female sex worker and a client, and each group can have its own number of partners (n_contacts as a dict). With match=, a share of partnerships is between people at the same level of a property of people, such as a risk group.

Each partnership has a number of acts per step (acts), so a disease’s beta on it is a probability per act (see ss.peract), and the share of them in which a condom is used (condoms, 0 unless set, e.g. by a subclass in add_pairs()), which a modifier of the transmission on the network reads, e.g. ss.modify(hiv.infection, efficacy=0.8, share=lambda net: net.edges.condoms, route='sexualnet').

A subclass that overrides step() forms its own partnerships: the base starts none for it, and has none of the parameters for forming them, only between.

For several types of partnership with their own rules, see STIsim; for partnerships that are not sexual (e.g. needle sharing), ss.DegreeNet.

Note: not CRN safe when people are born or die.

    ages=None,

Mixing between groups from a contact matrix; returns ss.MixingPools

A matrix, and what it is over:

  • a country’s, by name (ss.contacts('Kenya')), from starsim_data, by 5-year age group;
  • its own age groups, given by their lower edges (ss.contacts(matrix, ages=[0, 15, 65])), which need not be the model’s (it is regrouped to them);
  • the model’s own stratifications: its age groups (ss.contacts(matrix)), another one (ss.contacts(matrix, dims='risk')), or several together (ss.contacts(matrix, dims=['sex', 'risk'])), for mixing that is not a product, e.g. men mix with women, and by risk differently for each sex.

Mixing that is a product over stratifications takes a matrix for each, by name (ss.contacts(dict(age=..., area=...))): the contacts between two groups are the product of the entries for each. The matrix by age can have its own age groups here too (ss.contacts(dict(age=..., area=...), ages=[0, 15, 65])).

S, I, R = ss.states(3)
sir = ss.Disease(infection=S >> I @ 0.05*I, recovery=ss.Transition(I, R, dur=10), init_prev=0.01)

# A product: by age, then 90% of contacts within one's own area

fisher_yates_shuffle

networks.fisher_yates_shuffle(arr, randvals)

In-place Fisher-Yates shuffle using precomputed uniform random values.

Equivalent to np.random.permutation (a uniform random shuffle), but ~1.3x faster for the large arrays used in network generation. randvals should be uniform on [0, 1) and the same length as arr.

Note: randvals is consumed in order (randvals[k] for the k-th swap), not indexed by position. This means that changing the array length (e.g. adding one agent) shifts every swap, so the shuffle is not random-number safe – matching ss.RandomNet(method='exact')’s intended behavior (see ss.RandomNet(method='safe') for the CRN-safe version).

nb_transmit

networks.nb_transmit(src, trg, rel_trans, rel_sus, beta, randvals)

Optimized transmission kernel: returns the (source, target) UIDs of transmitting edges.

Fuses the gather/multiply/compare and the UID extraction into a single branchless pass. This avoids the full-length boolean temporary and the two separate gather passes (trg[mask], src[mask]) of the previous approach – ~1.4x faster at typical transmission rates, more when transmission is common. UIDs are emitted in edge order, preserving CRN behavior.
