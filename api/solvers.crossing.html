solvers.crossing

solvers.crossing

Not for the user: diseases whose states are tracked jointly in a compartmental run

It exists since a condition in one disease on a state of another (e.g. coinfection) needs the people counted by both.

Used with: compartments; declared modules

Crossing

solvers.crossing.Crossing(sim, names, reasons=None)

Diseases whose states are tracked jointly in a compartmental run, since a condition in one refers to a state of another (e.g. ss.modify(hiv.rel_trans, multiply=3, where=syph.I))

Each disease in the crossing is a dimension of the sim, named after it, whose levels are its compartments and which applies to the people of the others. Each keeps its own engine, which works out its rates and its results, and sees the people as it has them: by its own compartments, and by groups that include the compartments of the others (with its own dimension at its first level).

They are run by one program: its compartments are the product of the diseases’ compartments of people (and, for a compartment that is not people, such as a reservoir, one for each combination of the others’), and its groups are those of the sim’s other dimensions. Each flow of a disease is a flow of the program for each combination of the others’ compartments, so every rate is worked out from the people as they are at the start of the step (not each disease in turn), and a whole run, many runs, a particle filter, and a run in JAX take a crossing as they take one disease. The solvers that draw whole events in steps draw each disease’s in turn, from those rates, so each disease’s moves (e.g. through the stages of a dwell time) are drawn as they would be for it alone. The program’s compartments (y) are the one copy of the people: each engine’s (engine.y) are a view of them (Joint), so a change made in Python (e.g. by an intervention) is seen by all of them at once. The crossing is run as one engine is (see ss.solvers.Compartmental.step() and run_all(), where it is the engine’s owner): the last disease to step takes the step for all of them, and the first to take a whole run takes it for all of them, and gives each its part. Its size is counted before it is made (see ss.solvers.choose.joint_layout()), and kept within ss.options.max_joint; sim.describe() shows it, with the memory a whole run takes.

build
solvers.crossing.Crossing.build(programs=None)

The program of the diseases together (see the class docstring), from the program of each

The layout is worked out here, and again only if an engine’s program is made again (e.g. when a branch probability changes); the numbers, which can change from step to step or run to run, are taken from the engines’ by numbers(), from where each is in an engine’s program (self.pieces). Returns the program.

solvers.crossing.Crossing.init_layout()

Where each engine’s compartments and flows, in each of its groups, are in the program’s

A compartment of people of an engine, in a group (with the engine’s own dimension at its first level), is the program’s compartment for it and the others’ compartments that are the levels of their dimensions in that group, in the program’s group for the levels of the other dimensions. For each engine, idx gives where each of its compartments, in each of its groups, is in the program’s compartments by groups (flattened), or -1 for the groups it keeps empty; fidx does the same for its transitions and movers.

Joint

solvers.crossing.Joint(cross, idx)

An engine’s compartments by its groups (engine.y), when its disease is tracked jointly with others: a view of the compartments of their program (Crossing.y), so there is one copy of the people. Reading some of them gathers them, and writing them (e.g. an intervention that moves people) changes the program’s, which every engine then sees. A group that the engine keeps empty (see Crossing.init_layout()) reads as nobody, and writing to it changes nothing.
