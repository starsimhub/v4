solvers.compartmental

solvers.compartmental

Not for the user: the compartmental engine: a declared model run as groups of people (ode, binomial, gillespie, and tau-leaping)

It exists so that a model with nothing per person can run faster than as agents, from the same declaration.

Used with: compartments; declared modules

Classes

Name Description
Compartmental Runs a graph as compartments: deterministically (ode) or stochastically

Compartmental

solvers.compartmental.Compartmental(module, sim, solver=None)

Runs a graph as compartments: deterministically (ode) or stochastically (binomial, gillespie, or tau, the exact solver’s fallback for long runs)

It exists so that the deterministic and stochastic forms share one engine.

The states are expanded into compartments (one per branch and sub-stage) and the transitions into a flat list of flows, each with a source compartment, a rate, and the compartments it feeds. Every solver steps the same list of flows.

aging_rates
solvers.compartmental.Compartmental.aging_rates(strat)

The rate per year at which people age out of each age group (but the last): the density of people in the year below its upper edge, over the number in the group

With a given split (ages uniform within each group), this is one over its width; with a named population, or the world’s as a guess, it is from that pyramid, as agents age (so a group followed by a smaller one does not empty too fast).

binomial_substeps
solvers.compartmental.Compartmental.binomial_substeps()

The substeps the chain-binomial solver takes in each step, from the fastest total rate out of a compartment of people at the start (see substeps())

People who arrive in a compartment within a substep leave it from the next, so a compartment holds about x/2 too many, with x its total rate out times the substep, and R is that much too high (TC1). So x is kept to binomial_bias (5%), which bounds the bias in each compartment to about 2.5%; and near the epidemic threshold, where the prevalence moves by the bias in R over R0 - 1 (an SIS’s endemic prevalence is 1 - 1/R0), to 0.1 × |R0 - 1|, for a bias of about 5% (TB3: TBsim’s resistance model, +12% at 3 substeps a month, is +3% at 12; an SIS with R0 = 1.05 gave 0.083 for 0.048 at whole steps). A fixed delay doesn’t count, since its people don’t leave at a rate: a model whose only exits are fixed delays takes whole steps, which keeps its final size but lowers its peak (by about 15% for an SIR at a fifth of the delay a step); substeps=3 gives about 5%, for about twice the time.

substeps
solvers.compartmental.Compartmental.substeps()

The number of substeps to take in each timestep (0 to let the solver choose)

The chain-binomial solver takes enough substeps that its step bias is small, as far as the rates are known at the start (at most max_binomial): see binomial_substeps(). Each substep is off the ODE by an amount of order rate × dt (ST55: an acute stage of 1.7 months at monthly steps took everyone infected from 9% to 51%). It moves people through as many stages of a dwell time as they pass in a substep (see ss.kernels.draw_chain()), so it is the dwell’s mean that counts, not its stages; a fixed delay is a conveyor of whole steps, so its length doesn’t count. substeps=1 takes whole steps, as most chain-binomial models (and frameworks’ discrete-time models) do.
