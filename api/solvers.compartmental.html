solvers.compartmental

solvers.compartmental

Not for the user: the compartmental engine: a declared model run as groups of people (ode, ide, binomial, gillespie, and tau)

It exists so that a model with nothing per person can run faster than as agents, from the same declaration.

Used with: compartments; declared modules

Classes

Name Description
Compartmental Runs a graph as compartments: deterministically (ode, ide) or stochastically

Compartmental

solvers.compartmental.Compartmental(module, sim, solver=None)

Runs a graph as compartments: deterministically (ode, ide) or stochastically (binomial, gillespie, or tau)

It exists so that the deterministic and stochastic forms share one engine.

The states are expanded into compartments (one per branch and sub-stage) and the transitions into a flat list of flows, each with a source compartment, a rate, and the compartments it feeds. Every solver steps the same list of flows.

aging_rates
solvers.compartmental.Compartmental.aging_rates(strat)

The rate per year at which people age out of each age group (but the last): the density of people in the year below its upper edge, over the number in the group

With a given split (ages uniform within each group), this is one over its width; with a named population, or the world’s as a guess, it is from that pyramid, as agents age (so a group followed by a smaller one does not empty too fast).

binomial_substeps
solvers.compartmental.Compartmental.binomial_substeps()

The substeps the chain-binomial solver takes in each step, from the fastest total rate out of a compartment of people at the start (see substeps())

People who arrive in a compartment within a substep leave it from the next, so a compartment holds about x/2 too many, with x its total rate out times the substep, and R is that much too high. So x is kept to binomial_bias (5%), which bounds the bias in each compartment to about 2.5%; and near the epidemic threshold, where the prevalence moves by the bias in R over R0 - 1 (an SIS’s endemic prevalence is 1 - 1/R0), to 0.1 × |R0 - 1|, for a bias of about 5% (a model of TB resistance, +12% at 3 substeps a month, is +3% at 12; an SIS with R0 = 1.05 gave 0.083 for 0.048 at whole steps). A fixed delay doesn’t count, since its people don’t leave at a rate: a model whose only exits are fixed delays takes whole steps, which keeps its final size but lowers its peak (by about 15% for an SIR at a fifth of the delay a step); ss.solvers.binomial(substeps=3) gives about 5%, for about twice the time.

As agents, an edge transmits at most once to its susceptible end in a step, with every factor of its hazard in the exponent (see ss.Network.transmit()), so the force of infection through K contacts per unit time is K·(1 − e^(−b·D))/D, not K·b (see ss.solvers.Mixing.edge_dur()). Each flow by contact has its own exposures here (see flatten_rates()), so b is that flow’s own: the coefficient of the exposure’s state in its rate (with a beta for each route, that route’s, given as beta), times the susceptibility of each group, its modifiers, and the mean infectiousness of the state. A flow from several source states takes the factor of each, not that of their mean. It is worked out each step, so a beta that varies in time is followed.

Whether the program from the last step still holds, so the rates of this one need not be worked out again

A module with a step of its own stops a run being taken whole, but usually changes nothing the rates depend on. So, if nothing else needs Python between the steps (kernel_reason()), the only thing that can change them is a parameter set by that module, which is looked for at each step, as is the amount of a modifier. substeps
solvers.compartmental.Compartmental.substeps()

The number of substeps to take in each timestep (0 to let the solver choose)

The chain-binomial solver takes enough substeps that its step bias is small, as far as the rates are known at the start (at most max_binomial): see binomial_substeps(). Each substep is off the ODE by an amount of order rate × dt (an acute stage of 1.7 months at monthly steps can take everyone infected from 9% to 51%). It moves people through as many stages of a dwell time as they pass in a substep (see ss.kernels.draw_chain()), so it is the dwell’s mean that counts, not its stages; a fixed delay is a conveyor of whole steps, so its length doesn’t count. ss.solvers.binomial(substeps=1) takes whole steps, as most chain-binomial models (and frameworks’ discrete-time models) do.

tables
solvers.compartmental.Compartmental.tables(start=0, stop=None)

The parts of the program that can change over time, at each step (or as a single entry, if nothing varies)

A value that varies in time is worked out for every step at once. Modifiers that depend only on time are applied to the steps between the times they change, each span worked out at once in the same way; only a modifier that could change at any step (e.g. one that follows a smooth time series) is worked out step by step. The tables of a whole run are kept for as long as everything they are worked out from is the same, so a sim that is run again, with the same values, does not work them out again.
